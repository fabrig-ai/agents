# Leads

Provisional, 2026-10-28. A lead is a Claude session (Opus 5.5, at high effort) for one area (skein, smith, jig or temper), started for one part of the design: it turns that part into implementer tasks, keeps its implementers busy, and marks branches ready for the merge queue. It is ephemeral: it starts warm from its area's files and leaves everything that outlasts it in them (section 4). Read `AGENTS.md` first, then this file, `agents/operations.md` and `agents/ledger.md`.

## 1. In one page

- **Ephemeral, warm from files:** a lead lives for one part (at most one wave) of its area; what outlasts it is in its area's `common.md` and `state.md` and in the designs, never only in its context (section 4).
- **Claude is spent on the code's shape:** layout, names, types, APIs, boundaries, test worlds, task sketches, and temper's `docs/help/` pages (section 3). Everything else, implementation, rebases, conflicts, surveys and reviews included, is a Codex task: never a Claude subagent.
- **Names:** the lead's mailbox is `lead/<area>`; its branches are `<area>/<task>`, ready as `ready/<area>/<task>`.
- **A lead fans out:** several implementers at once, one task and one slot each, one per crate of the wave (three or four to start, as slots allow): its fixed share of slots, and any idle pool slot it claims itself. Run installed `claim` from PATH on hel8, not from the lead's clone: `claim list --free lead/<area>`, then `claim claim HOST N lead/<area> <task>`, and `claim release HOST N lead/<area>` when the task's report is in. Another lead's share goes only by that owner's agreed lend (`--lent-by SHARE_OWNER`); shares live in main's `tools/slots/hosts`.
- **A pipeline:** no slot waits; each report is handled in one turn; the first task of a part is out within 10 minutes of the part arriving.
- **One writer per crate:** a task owns its crates for the wave; a change another crate's interface needs is a question to the coordinator, which makes it the next interface step.
- **The meeting point first:** one thin story that runs both real sides of a boundary, as soon as the far side answers.
- **No landing work:** the merge queue merges; a lead marks a branch ready once its report, a skim of its diff and its precheck (section 2) say it's done, from its `repo/`: `git fetch origin && git push origin <sha>:refs/heads/ready/<area>/<task>`; it repairs what the queue sends back.
- **Reports up:** each part's tasks, what landed, what the code taught (for the design and its status file), and questions, to the coordinator (`s/dynamic-memory@hel8`): `post send --as lead/<area> s/dynamic-memory@hel8 SUBJECT` with the body on stdin, one line per event, a question first with its options and a recommendation.

## 2. Tasks

- A task is one file, read literally. Its first line says: read `agents/implementer.md` first; its departures from it are stated.
- It holds: the goal in 2-3 lines; the design sections to read, never a whole design; sketches of the outer layer (modules, names, public types and signatures, what crosses a boundary, the tests' shape); test sketches in the named world; acceptance criteria; the slot, the base commit, the branch, the files in scope, and the log's path.
- Check the sketches against main: every name and file they assume exists.
- `common.md` holds the standing rules every task names (fakes, fixtures, what not to touch), and grows when a question shows a gap. A task inherited from an older source is rewritten for fabrig's paths, slots, branches and queue before it goes out.
- The goal is no questions: tasks grow from small and certain; a question is answered in a short task file and the run resumed, and the gap closed in `common.md`.
- **Scope is settled in the task, not by questions.** Before writing a task, the lead runs (or reuses) a reverse-dependency survey of who reads the types, functions and files it changes. The task lists the files and crates it owns; the reader and neighbour files it may also change, and how (call sites only; a literal’s new field); and the likely options, each pre-approved with a default ("if X, do A; if Y would need changing, do B and say so in the report"). The implementer asks only beyond that list.
- **Size:** a feature task aims at about 20-30 implementer-minutes (all its runs) as soft guidance: one coherent change that one run finishes, with adjacent small items grouped by the lead. Same-kind small repairs (one family of selectors, one fixture class) go out as one task. At today's rate that is about 100-150 production added lines with their tests, recalibrated weekly from measurements. Exempt kinds, named in the task, are interface (expand), contraction, repair, rebase and docs; a feature task under about 50 production lines says why. The metric is the lead overhead ratio: the lead's time outside the implementer runs, up to the task's ready mark, over implementer minutes, with a median of at most 0.25; round trips at most 1. Implementation minutes and check minutes are reported separately; both count inside implementer minutes. Retain both the ratio and total cost. The measures are in director decisions 0024, 0030 and 0032 (records `director/decisions/0024.md`, `director/decisions/0030.md` and `director/decisions/0032.md`); the checks and repair grouping follow 0034 (`director/decisions/0034.md`).
- **The slot is trimmed first:** `codex-run` trims a writable task's slot before Codex starts (`agents/operations.md` 3); when it could not, the task's first step is `tools/slots/trim`.
- **Prechecked before ready** (the user, 10-29 and decision 0034): the last steps run within the implementer’s lifetime, in its own claimed, warm slot, with no other run using it: `git fetch origin`, then installed `precheck SLOT EXACT_TIP` (optionally `--base origin/main`). It merges the tip with main without committing, runs the queue's own selection (ratchets, the tools' suites when `tools/` changed, fmt, clippy, a test build) through ordinary `heavy`, and restores the checkout. Require exit 0, `outcome=passed` and `restored=true`; the implementer reports the exact checked tip, main base and checked merged tree OIDs, logs, and the original, unedited `result.json` receipt path and SHA256. Keep original failed receipts too; after grouped repairs, rerun the exact-tip precheck and readers’ tests. Precheck never requests an exclusive maintenance lease against its own live shared lease; it fails closed on unknown child processes or uncertain restoration. A merge conflict is a rebase task, a failed check a repair, an infrastructure failure a look at its log; a slot it leaves unresolved stays claimed and untouched until the operator reconciles it (`tools/queue/precheck.md`, 4). The implementer then runs the readers' tests below, aborts the merge and restores the branch clean at the exact checked tip. After its diff skim, the lead marks ready from that report, naming the checked tip, main commit, result path and log path. It launches a separate check run only if the branch changed after the run or the report lacks a check. Keep the claim held from task launch through ready; the lead owns ready-check, the ready ref and the claim’s release after ready.
- **A change of meaning runs its readers' tests:** a branch that changes what a shared item means, not only its signature (a value it returns, an order it keeps), also runs the tests of every package that reads that item, merged with main, before ready: a precheck only compiles. As the task's last steps after precheck, the implementer recreates the recorded main base merged with the exact checked tip (`git merge --no-ff --no-commit`, no second fetch or commit). Derive and retain the candidate readers from Cargo’s reverse dependency graph for the changed crate on that merge, including relevant dependency kinds and target configuration, default and all features; the implementer runs every candidate reader's tests through `heavy`, default then fuzzy, serially, never a subset filtered by grep or by name. A missing required reader cannot certify completeness. Report the candidate list and every reader’s outcome and per-package counts: nonzero default counts or a source-cited exclusion, and any fuzzy no-case result recorded as such. Then abort the merge and restore the branch clean at the checked tip. A hand-picked package list cannot prove coverage (ledger121).
- **Fresh at the push:** right before pushing `ready/`, run installed `ready-check RESULT --repo REPO --tip EXACT_TIP` (`tools/queue/precheck.md`), RESULT the precheck's own `result.json`, unedited, and EXACT_TIP the full OID it checked, then push that OID. Its exit 0 (`passed`) says main has changed no file the precheck touched since its base; a refusal (`rerun-precheck`) means fetch origin in the slot, precheck the same tip again and check its new result.
- **Disk admission for every heavy command:** the floor stays 80000000000 bytes. Until ops’ decision-0033 enable proof for that host, check `df -B1 --output=avail /`; below the floor, stop and report, keeping the claim. After that host is enabled, submit to `heavy` without a manual `df` refusal and wait on its bounded disk queue, up to 1200 accumulated below-minimum seconds. At the cap, report heavy’s capacity receipt as an infrastructure result, never a test red. At most one precheck of a lead’s per host runs at a time.
- **Counted baselines are the queue's:** tasks need not lower `scripts/old-forms.txt`, and a count never rises (`agents/operations.md` 5).
- **A read-only run cannot fetch:** move the survey clone to the commit it reads before launching it, and name that commit in the task (`agents/operations.md` 4).
- **Bind the check, not the syntax,** for tool configuration (coordinator.md, 3).
- **A branch stays in its tree,** but for a required field (coordinator.md, 3): the producer's branch carries the other tree's lines that name the field, each edited only in the field's name, type path or value, with values from that tree's lead, who agrees to the exact downstream diff before ready (a disagreement goes to the coordinator); the precheck covers every touched tree, and the downstream readers' tests run on the checked tip merged with the same main, the slot claimed through them.
- **Effort:** implementers run at high by default, medium for mechanical work (renames, repetitive edits, removing dead code). Surveys and investigations are read-only Codex runs (`codex-run task --read-only`), at medium, or high when the question is subtle.
- After two failed tries at the same task, a read-only Codex run advises before the third.

Every task follows this skeleton; fill its markers with the settled scope and commands:

```markdown
+++
task = "@TASK@"
branch = "@BRANCH@"
coordinator = "@COORDINATOR@"
draft = "@DRAFT@"
area = "@AREA@"
part = "@PART@"
+++
Read `AGENTS.md`, `agents/implementer.md` and `@TREE@/AGENTS.md` first. Departures: @DEPARTURES@.
## Goal
@GOAL_AND_ACCEPTANCE_CRITERIA@
## Inputs
Slot: @SLOT@; central claim: @OWNER@, WHAT @WHAT@; base: @BASE@; branch: @BRANCH@.
Report: @REPORT_PATH@; log: @LOG_PATH@; design sections and common rules: @INPUT_PATHS_AND_SECTIONS@.
## Owns and may change
@OWNED_FILES_AND_CRATES@; @READER_AND_NEIGHBOUR_FILES_AND_ALLOWED_EDITS@; @PREAPPROVED_OPTIONS_AND_DEFAULTS@.
## Steps
1. Verify the claim; trim if needed; check out the base and branch in place.
2. @IMPLEMENTATION_AND_TEST_SKETCHES@; narrow tests, fmt and clippy; commit and push @BRANCH@ only.
3. Keep the 80000000000-byte floor: before host enable proof, check df, stop/report below it and keep claim; after proof, submit without manual df refusal; heavy queues up to 1200 accumulated below-minimum seconds, then report its capacity receipt as infrastructure, never test red.
4. Within your lifetime, in your claimed slot, fetch origin; run installed `precheck @SLOT@ @EXACT_TIP@`; require exit 0, outcome=passed, restored=true; retain original receipt path/SHA256/logs, including failed receipts. Never request exclusive maintenance against your live shared lease; fail closed on unknown children or uncertain restoration.
5. ## Not yours
The lead owns ready-check, the ready ref and claim release.
## Report
First line `PASSED: @RESULT@` or `Question: @OPTIONS_AND_RECOMMENDATION@`; exact tip, main base and checked merged tree OIDs, production/test diff stat, original precheck receipt path/SHA256/logs and failed receipts, graph candidates, every reader’s outcome and default/fuzzy counts, clean restoration, implementation and check minutes separately within implementer minutes.
```

## 3. Watching the work

- Read implementers' logs every 30 minutes and step in where one is lost.
- A report gets a skim of the diff, not a review; a task that asked too much is discarded, and the next is smaller or fuller.
- **Skim, never dump:** what a lead reads stays in its context until it ends. A diff is read by `git diff --stat` and then the hunks in question; a log by `grep` or `tail` around the failure, never whole; a report by its first lines and the section asked about; a long survey or diff whose gist is enough goes to a read-only Codex run for a summary. Build and test output stays in its log file, named in a post, never streamed into the session.
- `docs/help/` pages (temper's, compiled into its tests) change with the behaviour, in the same branch, written by the lead, never by an implementer.

## 4. A lead's lifetime and its files

- **Started only by the coordinator, never by itself:** from `~/src/rust/tmp/supervise/leads/<area>` (a directory Claude trusts), `claude --bg --effort high --name lead/<area> "<prompt>"`, the prompt naming, by absolute path, `AGENTS.md`, this file, `agents/operations.md` and `agents/ledger.md` in the area's `repo/` (after a `git fetch` and a look at main's), and the area's `brief.md`, `common.md` and `state.md`. Before an area's first lead, the coordinator creates its `common.md` and `state.md` (empty lists); every later lead inherits them. Its first acts: read them; reconcile `state.md` with what is true (each intent, each run in flight by its report or its run's directory, each slot by `claim list`, each survey clone's commit, each ready branch by `git ls-remote`), reading again the mail its predecessor read after `state.md`'s last update (delivered copies stay in `~/src/rust/tmp/post/lead+<area>/cur/`); `post read lead/<area>`; then the next action `state.md` names, or the brief's first task.
- **Its scratch,** `~/src/fabrig/leads/<area>/` on hel8, outlives it:
  - `repo/` (its clone: read main, mark branches ready) and `survey/` (read-only runs);
  - `brief.md`, the coordinator's: the current part, its design sections, its order and its slots;
  - `common.md`, at the scratch's root: the area's standing rules and lasting facts (what every task names, inherited sources and their locations, measurement methods, what each question taught); it grows and is never reset between leads;
  - `state.md`, the lead's own and the one file its successor needs to carry on: per unfinished task, its full run id, host, slot or survey clone, base, branch, task, report and log paths, status (intended, out, reported, ready, landed, rejected, withdrawn) and next action; slots held and lends; ready branches waiting on the queue; questions pending and who answers each; what comes next in the part. Empty lists are written as empty, and each update replaces the file whole (a temporary file renamed over it). A task leaves `state.md` once landed or withdrawn and its run has ended and its slot is released; what it taught goes to `common.md` or to the coordinator;
  - task files (each also copied to its builder, `agents/operations.md` 2) and reports.
- **`state.md` changes with every event,** an event being anything that changes what is in flight or who waits on whom:
  - a launch or a resume of a run, a slot's claim, release or lend, and a branch marked ready, withdrawn or rebased under a new name: written as intended before the command and as done after it, so a lead stopped between the two leaves an intent its successor checks before acting. A launch's intent names its host, task and report path, which are unique, so a run whose id was lost is found by its report path in `codex-run`'s runs;
  - a run's end (a report, a question, a failure, an end with no report), the queue's landing or rejection, a question sent or answered (the coordinator, another lead, the operator): written as it is handled.

  In practice every pass of the mailbox loop ends with `state.md` true.
- **The mailbox outlives the session:** `post wait lead/<area>` runs in the background; on each end, `post read lead/<area>`, act, start it again. Mail sent while no lead runs waits in the box, so leads need no warm-up overlap (`agents/operations.md` 7 is for the coordinator and the operator).
- **What passes to the successor as it is:** the runs in flight with the crates they own (their ends post to `lead/<area>`; the successor handles their reports before giving overlapping work), the slot claims (owned by `lead/<area>`, released as each run's report comes in), the survey clones in use (never moved while their run lives), and the ready branches in the queue.
- **It ends** when its part is done (every task landed, withdrawn, or handed to an owner who has acknowledged it), when the coordinator asks it to end, or at `agents/operations.md` 7's threshold, measured, never guessed (`session-context`, installed on every machine (from the agents repository), run from the lead's start directory with CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID set, or with an explicit session id when several sessions have written there in the last 30 minutes, prints this session's usage and exits 3 at the threshold; a lead runs it after each event it handles): at 85% of its context, or right after a compaction if one comes first, never after a second. It brings `state.md` and `common.md` up to date, sends the coordinator what the design should say, stops its waiter, posts the coordinator `files current` (saying whether its part is done), and from then on reads no mail and changes no file.
- **Then the coordinator,** on `files current`, stops that session by the id it recorded when it started it (`claude stop <id>`; a late message from an earlier lead never stops its successor) and:
  - **the part unfinished:** starts the successor at once, on the same `brief.md`; it carries on from each task's next action in `state.md`, while the runs in flight go on and their ends wait in the mailbox;
  - **the part done:** writes the area's next part into `brief.md` and starts a lead for it, or, with no part for the area, starts none: its mail waits until one is started.
- **A lead that ends without `files current`** (a crash, a stop, a session found not running): the coordinator checks the leads' sessions and mailboxes every 30 minutes and whenever a lead is silent past its runs' expected ends (no waiter, or unread mail older than 10 minutes, makes it look at the lead's progress: `agents/coordinator.md` 3); a lead missing while its part is unfinished, or stopped without `files current`, is replaced the same way, and its successor reconciles first, as above, before any new launch or release.
